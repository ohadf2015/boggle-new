## Design Context

### Users
Mixed casual and competitive audience — friends playing together for fun AND serious word gamers chasing rankings. Ages ~15-40, playing on phones (primary input) and TV/big screens (host/party mode). Context ranges from quick solo daily challenges on a commute to loud multiplayer party sessions with a shared screen. Supports 5 languages including Hebrew (RTL).

### Brand Personality
**Quirky, electric, loud.** Three pillars:
1. **Loud & playful** — Jackbox-style party energy, bold colors, maximum fun
2. **Slick & competitive** — satisfying feedback, clean game feel, skill matters
3. **Quirky & surprising** — personality-driven, witty mascot reactions, unexpected charm

The brand should feel like a word game that doesn't take itself seriously but takes its gameplay very seriously.

### Aesthetic Direction
**Neo-Brutalist, refined** — keep and polish the current direction:
- Dark navy backgrounds with halftone texture
- Hard pixel shadows (no blur), solid 2-3px black borders
- Electric color coding: lime (primary), pink (multiplayer), cyan (solo), purple (brain training)
- Fredoka display + Rubik body typography
- Kawaii marshmallow cube mascot with 20+ emotional states
- Press animations, pop entrances, satisfying micro-interactions
- RTL-aware shadows and layouts for Hebrew

**Anti-references:** Generic mobile game UI, soft gradients, glassmorphism, "corporate game" aesthetics.

### Design Principles
1. **Energy with intention** — Every bold choice (color, shadow, animation) should serve gameplay clarity, not just look cool
2. **Phone AND TV** — All UI must work on small touch screens and big party screens equally well
3. **Personality everywhere** — The mascot, copy, and interactions should surprise and delight; never generic
4. **Competitive clarity** — Scores, timers, and game state must be instantly readable even in chaotic multiplayer
5. **Coherent chaos** — Many modes, many colors, many features — but one unmistakable visual identity
