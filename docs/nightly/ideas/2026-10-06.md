# Competitor + viral concepts — 2026-10-06

## Concepts worth borrowing

- **Gimkit — missed-answer resurfacing (spaced repetition baked into the core loop)** — source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gimkit / https://www.differentiatedteaching.com/blooket-vs-gimkit/
  - Gimkit's defining edge over Blooket: items a student gets wrong come back around *more often* during the same session, not on a fixed interval. It's the game loop itself doing spaced repetition, not a separate "review mode".
  - Why it works: turns "I got this wrong" into immediate, low-friction retry pressure instead of shame — the miss becomes the next scoring opportunity, not a dead end.
  - How it fits LexiClash: `brain-drill` (admin-gated) is literally a drill mode and has no resurfacing logic today — words a player misses just vanish into the session log. Weighting the next-word queue toward recent misses is a direct lift.
  - Effort: S — the drill already tracks per-word correctness; this is a queue-selection change, no new UI.
  - Risk: low. Must not touch scoring/coin logic — purely which word is served next.

- **Blooket — collectible "Blooks" as a passive meta-layer over a dull core loop** — source: https://triviamaker.com/gimkit-alternatives/ / https://slideswith.com/blog/blooket-vs-kahoot-vs-gimkit-vs-quizizz
  - Blooket's quiz mechanic is plain multiple-choice; the entire retention hook is the character-collection meta sitting on top (correct answers earn currency, currency opens packs, packs are the actual reason kids ask to play again).
  - Why it works: decouples "is the round fun" from "do I want another round" — the collection habit survives a boring individual question.
  - How it fits LexiClash: `wheel-rush` has no standalone surface (0.15-weight MP-rotation pick only) and nothing persists across sessions today. A tiny passive-collectible layer (a "charm" earned per clean spin) gives it a reason to be the pick players hope for in rotation, without building a new mode.
  - Effort: M — needs a small persisted collection table + a reveal moment; no new route.
  - Risk: low-medium — must not become a second currency that touches `profiles.total_coins` (coin-economy hard line). Keep it cosmetic/charm-only, not convertible to coins.

- **r/wordgames "Letter Tangle" / "Kerners" — hidden-theme word clusters** — source: https://www.reddit.com/r/wordgames/comments/1wuzx3h/we_just_released_letter_tangle_a_hard_word_puzzle/
  - Mechanic: puzzles are built around a cryptic hidden theme; words you find connect to each other through shared letters/meaning, and the "click" of spotting the theme is the payoff, not just finding more words.
  - Why it works: surprise/discovery hook — same word-finding skill, but a second layer of pattern-recognition reward sits on top of the raw score.
  - How it fits LexiClash: `word-craft` (`?mode=cards`) rounds are currently score-only. A rotating secret category (3 found words share a hidden theme → reveal + bonus at round end) adds a surprise payoff without new UI chrome.
  - Effort: S — theme-tagging a subset of the existing word list + an end-of-round reveal card.
  - Risk: low. Theme list needs native-speaker spot-check per locale before shipping (translation risk, not logic risk).

- **Lexible.app — vote-on-rule-changes custom mode** — source: https://www.reddit.com/r/wordgames/comments/1wvkkx3/lexibleapp_classic_to_custom_progressive_hints/
  - Lets players vote mid-game on rule tweaks (extra tile, blank-swap ability). Niche but interesting social-customization angle.
  - Why it works: gives returning players agency over difficulty instead of a fixed global setting.
  - How it fits LexiClash: longer-term MP idea, not a tonight-sized slice — noted for the backlog, not pitched as tonight's polish (see report).
  - Effort: L. Risk: scope creep into MP settings surface — defer.

## What other word-game sites do well

- **r/wordgames front page (this week):** nearly every top post is a solo dev's "I made X, feedback?" — the sub rewards specific, concrete asks ("is the difficulty curve fair?", "post your times") over generic showcases. A stat-driven post ("29% of 5-letter words end in S, stop putting S last") out-performed most launches by giving readers something immediately useful and shareable — a reminder that a sharp, verifiable fact travels further than a feature list.
- **Gimkit/Blooket (education portals):** both platforms separate "the quiz is the same for everyone" from "the game wrapper is wildly different per mode" — 12+ Blooket modes reuse one question bank. LexiClash's admin-beta modes already do this (shared dictionary/solver), so the gap isn't content, it's the wrapper polish — consistent with founder priority #2 (mode readiness).
