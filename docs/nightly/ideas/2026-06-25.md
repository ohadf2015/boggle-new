### 1. VocabularySpellingCity Price Vacuum → LexiClash Education Pricing Anchor
- **Source:** https://spellingjoy.com/best-apps/spellingcity-alternatives + msn.com Blooket/Kahoot 2026 article
- **What it does:** SpellingCity rebranded to Vocabulary A-Z and hiked from ~$35/yr → $108–125/yr classroom license. Districts are dropping it en masse. Teachers are actively searching for replacements right now (mid-year budget cycle).
- **Why it works:** Price sensitivity in EdTech is extreme. Teachers have a $35/yr anchor in memory; anything ≤$60 feels like a win. The churn is creating Google-searchable intent ("SpellingCity alternatives 2026") that is peaking NOW.
- **How it fits LexiClash:** Education module lives at `/[locale]/education/for-schools`. A $49/class/year price point is exactly half SpellingCity's new price, covers 5 languages including Hebrew/Spanish (none of the alternatives do), and the existing multiplayer engine is more engaging than SpellingCity's flashcard loop. Proposed trial: 30 days free, then $49/year for up to 35 students. This also directly fixes the "free forever" copy issue the founder flagged.
- **Effort:** S — pricing copy + trial CTA, no new feature needed
- **Risk:** Trial → paid conversion depends on teacher retention; build a reminder email at day 25

### 2. Gimkit Spaced-Repetition "Wrong Resurface" → Word Vault Dead-End Map
- **Source:** https://wordifyr.com/blooket-or-gimkit/ + https://learnclash.com/blog/blooket-vs-gimkit
- **What it does:** Gimkit's killer differentiator vs. Blooket: questions answered incorrectly resurface more frequently. Students spend more time on the words they don't know. Result: measurable learning vs. just "playing."
- **Why it works:** The mechanic creates productive frustration — you can't avoid your blind spots. Retention goes up. Teachers notice it; it's the main reason Gimkit maintains a dedicated classroom user base that Blooket keeps chipping at.
- **How it fits LexiClash:** Word Vault mode (admin-gated). After 3+ failed combo attempts, render a small "dead-end list" — crossed-out chips of your wrong guesses in the corner. Not a hint, not a reveal — just "you've already tried FIRE+WATER, FIRE+EARTH." Eliminates random re-tapping, turns brute force into elimination logic. Same cognitive principle as Gimkit's spaced-resurface.
- **Effort:** S — useState tracking of failed combos + render ~~chips~~ below input
- **Risk:** Low; feature is additive and opt-in via existing mode flow

### 3. Blooket Cosmetics + Class Leaderboard → LexiClash Student Badge Economy
- **Source:** https://www.msn.com/en-us/news/other/blooket-surpasses-kahoot-in-2026-as-gimkit-and-kahoot-launch-major-updates + blooket.com
- **What it does:** Blooket surpassed Kahoot in 2026 partly by adding collectible "Blooks" (cosmetics) earned via gameplay currency. Students play to collect characters; teachers leverage social pressure ("who has the rarest Blook?") as a classroom engagement lever.
- **Why it works:** The cosmetic layer is the retention mechanic. The actual quiz/game is the delivery vehicle. Teachers adopt because students self-motivate. The 27-mode library also means teachers can rotate game format while keeping the same question set.
- **How it fits LexiClash:** Education classroom sessions could award milestone badges (first 5-word streak, first 10-word game, first Hebrew/Spanish word) that appear on the `/student` dashboard. Zero new economy — just achievement unlocks surfaced at the class-session level. Teacher dashboard at `/teacher` shows badge completion per student. Connects to existing coin/avatar system.
- **Effort:** M — badge schema + session-end award check + teacher view
- **Risk:** Medium; requires DB schema + RLS. Queue as follow-up to pricing change.

### 4. BGG Sealed-Bid-With-Cancellation → Showdown "Matching Words Cancel" Rule
- **Source:** https://boardgamegeek.com/boardgamemechanic/2920/auction-sealed-bid + Founder directive (06-24 poker-feel + "best word nobody thought of")
- **What it does:** In sealed-bid-with-cancellation (classic board game mechanism): if ≥2 players bid identically, ALL those bids are eliminated — the highest non-duplicated bid wins. Creates strong incentive to be unique.
- **Why it works:** It's the mechanical expression of "be different or lose." Forces players to model other players' thinking (theory of mind), which is what makes poker engaging. The optimal play is never the obvious play.
- **How it fits LexiClash:** Sealed Bid Showdown (lane 05 shipped the reveal stagger 06-25). Add the cancellation rule: if ≥2 players submit the same word, BOTH score zero for that round. Announce canceled pairs with a "Great minds..." animation. Suddenly "QUIZ" is risky if you think someone else will also play "QUIZ." Drives to the founder's "best word nobody thought of" daily-challenge variant.
- **Effort:** S — add dedup check to round scoring, emit canceled-pair event for animation
- **Risk:** Low; rule change, no schema migration needed

### 5. Typocalypse Audio-Spell → Brain Drill "Hear & Type" Mode
- **Source:** r/wordgames thread `1u9p8g9` (Typocalypse — "spell words when you hear an audio")
- **What it does:** Typocalypse includes a mode where players hear a word spoken aloud and must type it correctly. Pure listening-to-spelling flow. No visual letter hints.
- **Why it works:** Engages a completely different cognitive pathway than reading letters on a grid. Especially powerful for ESL/language learners where audio→spelling is the real-world skill. Also makes brain drills phone-friendly (you can close your eyes on transit).
- **How it fits LexiClash:** Brain Drill mode (admin-gated). Add a 🔊 TTS button to each drill card using `window.speechSynthesis.speak(word)` — zero new deps, Web Speech API is in every browser. Optional: hide the printed word until player types it (toggle "blind mode"). Doubles as ESL marketing hook ("practice spelling Hebrew/Spanish/Swedish words by ear").
- **Effort:** S — ~15 lines, single component
- **Risk:** Low; Web Speech API support is >96% globally. Hebrew TTS is available in all major browsers via the `he-IL` voice.

## What other word-game sites do well

- **playlinx.app** — hub branding ("hand-crafted, no ads, no spam") converts trust-fatigued players. No ads is a feature they market first, game quality second. LexiClash could learn: on edu pages, lead with "no ads in classroom mode."
- **joggleland.net** — 25-year-old Boggle site with active community and "all words you missed" post-game review. Post-game miss-review is retention glue; LexiClash's game summaries don't yet show "words you missed" systematically.
- **Centumth** — inverse-target daily (aim for #100, not #1) has a built-in social share hook: sharing your score is inherently interesting because everyone's chasing a different sweet spot. For Word Forge's "Precision Target" (already in BUILD ledger), same principle applies.
- **SpellingJoy.com** — aggressively targeting "SpellingCity alternatives 2026" with SEO. LexiClash's `/education/for-schools` should add a section addressing this comparison before SpellingJoy owns the keyword.
