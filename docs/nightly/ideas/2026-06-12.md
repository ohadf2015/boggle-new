# Competitor + viral concepts — 2026-06-12

## Concepts worth borrowing

- **Chained Ending Constraint (LinkLot)** — source: https://playlinklot.com/
  - Players form a chain of 6 five-letter words; each word's final letter becomes the first letter of the next; all six ending letters must be UNIQUE, so you must plan the full chain before committing to word 1.
  - Why it works: forced long-horizon planning inside a simple letter rule. "I boxed myself in on word 3" is a natural share moment. The constraint is intuitive but the depth isn't obvious until you fail.
  - LexiClash fit: a "Chain Sprint" daily variant — pick 4-letter words (suits the board), chain 5 links, ending-letter uniqueness constraint tracked live with a strip of "used enders." Reuses tile-drag + dict; just adds a chain validator and planning strip UI.
  - Effort: S (new scoring constraint + UI strip, no new dict needed)
  - Risk: Shiritori family adjacency — but the unique-ending constraint + planning horizon make it clearly distinct mechanically

- **Clue Economy Scoring (elquo)** — source: https://www.elquo.co.uk
  - One hidden word; players start with 100 points; each clue purchased (letter reveal, position, "contains X") deducts 20 pts; finding the word keeps your remaining score. Goal: find it early WITH ZERO OR FEW clues. Score = certainty × frugality.
  - Why it works: reframes "getting help" from accessibility feature to a strategic loss. "I got it for 80 pts" is a status signal. OP's exact ask: "Does the first clue feel hard-but-fair, or just obscure?" — they're wrestling with difficulty calibration, which means the mechanic is still being validated.
  - LexiClash fit: "**Clue Budget Daily**" — one hidden target word on the 4×4 daily board; 100 pts to start; clue types reuse existing hint infrastructure (first/last letter, word length, "appears in row X"); share card "Found it for 60 pts — 2 clues." Mechanic family: clue-economy precision (new to ledger).
  - Effort: S — daily target seeder + clue-cost deduction + share card; existing hint infrastructure is already ad-gated, this repurposes it as a scoring variable
  - Risk: low; no trademark exposure; directly differentiates from Wordle clones that give hints for free

- **Mechanic Rotation Daily (Clu3)** — source: https://clu3.app (inferred from r/wordgames post `1u167k5`)
  - One hidden word, three clues (word + emoji + image); the key twist: every day the META-rule changes — some days clues point directly at the answer, some days they point away from it, some days they're fragments you assemble. The meta-puzzle is inferring what TYPE of day it is.
  - Why it works: adds a layer of inference about the puzzle itself, not just the answer. Players discuss "is today a misdirection day?" — community engagement beyond sharing results.
  - LexiClash fit: "**Rule Flip Daily**" — the scoring modifier changes each day (day A: only 5+ letter words count; day B: palindrome-eligible words score 2×; day C: no 3-letter words); players infer the rule from feedback on their first 2-3 words then exploit it. Share card: "Cracked the rule on word 2." Mechanic family: rule-inference (new to ledger; distinct from constraint-deduction and hidden-pair-discovery).
  - Effort: S — daily rule seed + 3-4 scoring modifier variants + rule-reveal animation on first violation
  - Risk: low; mechanic is original relative to the ledger

- **Quote Reconstruction (Tangled Texts)** — source: r/wordgames `1u31frg`
  - Famous quote with all words shuffled; player rearranges them; when two adjacent-in-the-original words are placed together they auto-merge into one draggable unit. Chain completions accelerate; final "snap" is satisfying.
  - Why it works: eliminates the blank-staring-at-jumble problem; tactile progress feedback; works in any language; cultural trivia layer (whose quote is this?).
  - LexiClash fit: "**Quote Chain Daily**" — same mechanic but seeded from a curated quote bank (100+ multilingual quotes pre-loaded). Works especially well for HE/JA/ES (famous quotes translate cleanly). Share card shows quote + time-to-solve.
  - Effort: M (quote DB + drag-merge UI is new, no solver needed but UX is novel)
  - Risk: copyright of quotes is fine for pre-1925 / CC; modern quotes need attribution

- **Sliding Puzzle × Word Hunt (TileHunt, Sliding Word)** — source: r/wordgames `1u2294i`, `1u0egvz`
  - Multiple devs launched this week: 7×7 grid + one empty slot; slide tiles into position (15-puzzle mechanic) then tap letters to form words in straight lines. Combines physical manipulation with vocabulary search.
  - Why it works: two distinct cognitive modes in sequence — spatial (slide) then verbal (find) — prevents the boredom curve of pure word search.
  - LexiClash fit: less direct — LexiClash's tile-drag is already the "manipulation" layer; but the insight worth borrowing is the TWO-PHASE structure (setup phase → scoring phase). Could inform a future Blast variant where players pre-arrange 3 tiles before the timer starts.
  - Effort: M-L for a full mode; S for borrowing the two-phase structure into an existing mode
  - Risk: multiple competitors shipped this exact mechanic this week — window to differentiate is narrow

## What other word-game sites do well

- **poki.com / crazygames.com** — prominent daily streak counters and "play in browser, no download" badge above the fold → convert fence-sitters who expect native-app friction. LexiClash has this but buries it.
- **Wordaround dev meta-contest** (r/wordgames `1u06bub`) — dev challenged players to beat his own high score with a $5 prize. Cost: $5. Result: organic front-page post with dozens of engaged replies. Revenue-positive virality trick worth copying for a daily leaderboard challenge week.
