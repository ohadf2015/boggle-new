# Competitor + viral concepts — 2026-11-18

## Concepts worth borrowing

- **Proxima-style adaptive word-difficulty engine** — source: https://www.educationalappstore.com/best-apps/our-5-best-apps-for-super-spelling
  - Spelling Shed's "Proxima" engine continuously assesses each student's spelling ability and feeds back words at their exact level instead of a fixed list.
  - Why it works: keeps both weak and strong spellers in their effort sweet spot — the #1 reason classroom tools get abandoned is a fixed list that's too easy or too hard for half the class.
  - Fit for LexiClash: Brain Drill (admin-gated, zero traffic per memory) is exactly the surface this helps — right now it's one fixed difficulty. Track per-student rolling accuracy and bias the next word's length/rarity accordingly.
  - Effort: M (needs a per-student rolling-accuracy column + a word-bucket selector; no new UI).
  - Risk: low — pure backend tuning, no new surface.

- **Class-level (not individual) leaderboard** — source: https://www.educationalappstore.com/best-apps/our-5-best-apps-for-super-spelling
  - Spelling Shed explicitly ranks schools/classes against each other, never individual kids within a class.
  - Why it works: removes the public-shame risk for the weakest speller in the room, which is the thing that makes a teacher stop using a competitive tool. The competitive pull stays (class vs. class) without a kid seeing "you're last."
  - Fit for LexiClash: word-craft `?mode=cards` classroom rounds — aggregate the round score to a class total shown on the shared screen; keep individual results private (student-only, in their own dashboard).
  - Effort: S (sum existing per-student round scores into a class total; no new scoring logic).
  - Risk: low.

- **Dordle-style simultaneous dual-board solve** — source: https://beebom.com/puzzle/games-like-wordle/
  - Dordle's hook is solving two Wordle grids at once with one shared guess budget — splits attention, raises perceived skill ceiling.
  - Why it works: a well-understood single-board game (Boggle/Wordle-likes) gets a second life by forcing split attention, without inventing a new core loop.
  - Fit for LexiClash: Brain Drill as a "Twin Drill" variant — two question boards side by side, one shared timer/life pool, only during admin-beta testing. Distinct mechanic family from the already-pitched "missed-word resurfacing" idea (2026-11-13, do not repeat that one).
  - Effort: M (duplicate the existing single-board component, share state for timer/lives).
  - Risk: low-medium — needs a layout check on small phones (two boards side by side).

## What other word-game sites do well

- CrazyGames' word category (`/c/word`) is dominated by zero-friction instant-play Boggle/word-search variants (Word Wipe, Word Fishing, Waffle Words) — no signup, no onboarding, straight into a board. LexiClash's own guest-play-first flow already matches this; worth protecting as new modes land (no login wall before first round).
- Spelling Bee - Games & Tests leans on "daily worldwide challenges" framing (a shared daily puzzle everyone is solving together) rather than private practice — reinforces that Daily mode's shared-seed framing is the right default, not a regression to defend against.
