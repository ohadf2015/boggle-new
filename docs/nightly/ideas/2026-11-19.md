# Competitor + viral concepts — 2026-11-19

## Concepts worth borrowing
- **Boss Spin / mutation round** — source: Beyond Words (GoldeneEye devs, Scrabble roguelike, PCGamer review) + Cursed Words (Rogueliker review)
  - Both 2026 indie word-roguelikes build tension by letting a round's board persist and escalate, then periodically drop a "boss" that mutates the rules (shrink the board, flood it with dead tiles, hike the target score) before the next normal round.
  - Works because: normal rounds feel mastered, then the boss spike resets the player's plan mid-run — classic surprise/variable-reward, and it's cheap (no new content, just a rule flip for one round).
  - Fit for LexiClash: an admin-beta MP mode (wheel-rush) that currently has zero "something different happens every N rounds" mechanic — see polish card below.
  - Effort: S (one conditional branch in the round-setup path + a banner telling players it's a boss round).
  - Risk: low — purely a rules/UI change, no new assets.

- **Streak-building synergy (carry a bonus forward only while you keep hitting)** — source: same Beyond Words/Cursed Words loop (board persists between rounds, letters you've already placed double-dip on later scoring).
  - Why it works: the reward isn't a flat multiplier, it's one the player *built* over consecutive hits, so losing it on a miss stings more than never having it (loss aversion > flat bonus).
  - Fit: word-craft gems — stack a per-category catch streak instead of the existing one-shot dice bonus / mystery-score ideas (those are session-random or class-level, not player-earned-in-the-moment). See polish card below.
  - Effort: S-M (a streak counter + multiplier display on the existing catch loop).
  - Risk: low.

- **Freemium-breadth over feature-depth for teacher acquisition** — source: SpellingJoy's Gimkit-alternatives roundup (Blooket/Quizizz free tiers vs Gimkit's paid-gated variety).
  - What it does: Blooket and Quizizz win teacher adoption by keeping most game-mode variety free and only gating capacity/reporting; Gimkit gates variety itself and converts fewer first-time teachers.
  - Why it works: a teacher's first session is a trust-building demo to their class — any paywall mid-demo kills the referral loop before it starts.
  - Fit for LexiClash education: our Pro gate should stay on capacity/reporting/class-management, never on which game mode a teacher can run in their first class — worth an audit of where the current Pro wall sits relative to this.
  - Effort: N/A (audit item, not a feature pitch) — flagging for whoever owns the education Pro gate.
  - Risk: none (no-code note).

- **Quizlet-style peer-content virality** — source: same SpellingJoy roundup.
  - What it does: Quizlet's growth comes almost entirely from millions of user-made flashcard sets being publicly searchable/shareable, not from Quizlet's own content team.
  - Fit: LexiClash's teacher-made word lists (classroom vocab decks) could become shareable/discoverable the same way — a teacher who shares a deck is also an acquisition channel. Already-shipped teacher/student infra (per memory) means this is mostly a "make existing lists public + searchable" change, not new data modeling.
  - Effort: M (needs a public list gallery + search, but reuses existing list data).
  - Risk: privacy — must stay school-safe (no student PII in shared decks); scope to teacher-authored word lists only.

## What other word-game sites do well
- Beyond Words / Cursed Words (Steam, 2026): both let the SAME board persist across rounds instead of resetting it, so earlier plays keep paying off — makes short rounds feel like they're building toward something instead of being disposable.
- SpellingJoy / classroom-review sites: every Gimkit-alternative comparison page converts on one line — "which is free vs which is paid" — confirming price-gate placement matters more than feature count for first-time teacher conversion.
