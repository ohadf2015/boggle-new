# Competitor + viral concepts — 2026-05-27

## Concepts worth borrowing

- **Lexo's card layer on word scoring** — source: https://lexo.gg/lobby (r/wordgames, score 7)
  - What it does: Scrabble-style scoring on a letter grid, but each player holds a hand of Uno-style action cards — play a card mid-round to block letters, steal bonus squares, or force an opponent to skip their longest word.
  - Why it works: two engagement systems running in parallel. Players who can't find long words still feel agency through card plays. Casual + strategic players both get a lever. The "card timing" decision layer is what keeps people thinking after the round ends.
  - LexiClash fit: a "sabotage card" hand between MP rounds (not during, which would cause chaos). You earn cards from round performance and spend them before the next board drops. Already have sabotage groundwork in Word Tower.
  - Effort: L. Risk: scope, balance.

- **Scrabbi's territory control** — source: r/wordgames thread (score 1, very new — early signal)
  - What it does: word game on a shared grid. The cells your word touches become "your" territory (colored). You're simultaneously finding words and contesting board position — like Scrabble crossed with Go.
  - Why it works: a found word has two outcomes (score + territory), so every move has a strategic dimension even when your vocabulary lead is slim. Trailing players can still win on board control.
  - LexiClash fit: new MP board variant where each found path claims cells. Player with most cells at timer end wins regardless of raw score. Would make Boggle-grid layout feel new.
  - Effort: L (new game-mode level work). Risk: UX complexity, balance.

- **Drift's progressive revelation** — source: playdrift.today (r/wordgames thread, organic praise)
  - What it does: given a modern English word, guess what it meant in 1400s-1600s. Hints are available but each hint costs you. The "aha" of realizing how much a word has drifted is the actual reward — the game teaches etymology while you play.
  - Why it works: variable reward + learning + cost tradeoffs = high engagement density. Players feel smart when they get it without hints. The curiosity gap drives daily return.
  - LexiClash fit: after finding a long word in Daily mode, show a brief etymology pop ("FLOUR comes from FLOWER — same word until 1650"). No mechanic change, pure content layer. Effort S, but curation is heavy.
  - Effort: S (UI trivial) + M (content curation). Risk: data source needed.

- **ReelWords' thematic identity** — source: reelwords.io (r/wordgames, fresh post)
  - What it does: fishing game where fish carry letters. You reel in the letters you want, spell words from your rack. Daily quests, leveling. Free browser, no signup.
  - Why it works: strong thematic memory hook — players remember "the fishing word game" before they remember the name. The non-word-game wrapper makes it socially describable. "You fish for letters" is a shareable pitch in one sentence.
  - LexiClash fit: existing modes lack individual theming that's memeable. "Word Tower" looks like construction but plays like Boggle. "Blast" could lean harder into the mining/explosion physicality. The thematic gap is largest in admin-gated modes that need to earn a place in the hub.
  - Effort: S (art + copy, no mechanic change). Risk: low.

- **Fluxis exodus = active acquisition window** — source: r/wordgames, multiple posts this week
  - What it does: scodtt's "Fluxis is Fluxed" + "Independent game results" threads have built a daily score-sharing community around indie word games. Players are bored with Fluxis (run by The Atlantic, quality declining) and actively organizing around indie alternatives.
  - Why it works: this is an organic "switching" community forming in real time. They already have daily play habits. They're just looking for better games to share scores on.
  - LexiClash fit: LexiClash appeared in the indie game results thread on 05-24 and 05-26. Keep showing up in each new installment with a score. This is the highest-leverage organic channel right now — cost zero, audience is warm.
  - Effort: S (human action, not code). Risk: none.

## What other word-game sites do well

- **Poki / CrazyGames portals**: featured placement requires mobile-first + clear "how to play" in 5 seconds. Games with no tutorial beyond the first move ("tap a letter") convert better than anything with a modal instructions screen. LexiClash's onboarding modal is a blocker here.
- **Lexamid (lexamid.com)**: clean daily puzzle with an endless mode. The endless mode is what converts casual visitors — having something to do after the daily is solved removes the biggest drop-off moment.
- **WordPVP (wordpvp.com)**: real-time tournament format where top 10% wins. Explicit ranking system (ELO ladder) creates a progression loop in competitive play. The "unlock longer words" progression is clever — you feel the game growing with you.
