# Competitor + viral concepts — 2026-05-29

## Concepts worth borrowing

- **Cross Bot (NYT Crossplay)** — source: https://techcrunch.com/2026/01/21/nyt-games-scrabble-like-game-crossplay-is-a-dream-come-true/
  - After every game, an AI reviews each move and shows the highest-scoring play that was available. You see exactly what you missed.
  - Why it works: regret is a return-hook. "I got 34 points but Cross Bot showed I could have gotten 61" is a guaranteed replay trigger. It's not punishment — it's aspirational.
  - LexiClash fit: post-game results screen already exists for MP and Blast. Adding a "best word you didn't play" line (pure client-side, from the same dictionary the game already validates against) would be S effort and very high emotional payoff. No server infra needed — just run solver over the board at game-end and surface the top-3 missed words.
  - Effort: S (solver already exists; results screen already rendered)
  - Risk: low — doesn't touch game balance, no new socket events

- **Connections Racer (connectionracer.com)** — source: https://www.connectionracer.com/
  - Same Connections puzzle (16 words → 4 categories), but all players solve simultaneously. First to sort all four groups wins.
  - Why it works: converts a solo daily into a live race without changing the puzzle itself. The competitive layer is entirely additive — same content, new tension.
  - LexiClash fit: "Daily Connections" race mode — one shared thematic puzzle per day, 2–4 players race to categorize. Reuses existing MP room + socket infra. Content creation is the real cost.
  - Effort: M (puzzle content creation + category-sorting UI)
  - Risk: medium — requires human-curated daily puzzles (or GPT pipeline)

- **Nanagrams built-in voice chat** — source: https://nanagrams.io/
  - 2–8 player simultaneous Bananagrams, free, browser, with WebRTC voice in private rooms. Players see each other's grids live.
  - Why it works: "kitchen table game" feeling in a browser tab. Removes the social barrier between casual and committed play. Rated 4.8/5, but English-only and no mobile.
  - LexiClash fit: LexiClash already supports multilingual + mobile. Adding voice to MP rooms would be a direct differentiator — especially for the Hebrew/Swedish player groups who already play together. WebRTC peer-to-peer = free at scale.
  - Effort: L (WebRTC + signaling server + moderation considerations)
  - Risk: medium — moderation complexity; RTL voice UI needs care

- **Wordrace letter-claiming scarcity (bloob.io/wordrace)** — source: https://bloob.io/wordrace
  - Shared grid, 1–12 players. Once a player claims a letter for a word, no one else can use it. Real-time scramble, all directions.
  - Why it works: creates genuine zero-sum tension on a shared resource. Every word your opponent plays shrinks your options. Speed + vocabulary depth both matter.
  - LexiClash fit: MP Classic already has a shared board. "Letter lock" variant where claimed tiles go dark would be a distinct mode modifier — S effort on top of existing grid rendering.
  - Effort: S (variant flag + tile-state broadcast)
  - Risk: low — additive to existing MP; can launch as modifier without a new mode

- **Undercover Word hidden-info asymmetry** — source: https://www.undercoverword.com/social-deduction-games
  - Most players share a secret word; one player gets a "neighboring" word (semantically close but different). Everyone gives one-word clues; the group must identify the undercover player before voting.
  - Why it works: bluffing + social deduction on vocabulary — you need to know what words are *close* to the real answer without accidentally confirming it. High replay (different undercover player each round).
  - LexiClash fit: party game mode — 4–8 players, 5 rounds, themed word pairs (e.g., "OCEAN" vs "LAKE"). Entirely new mechanic family. Requires curated word-pair lists per language.
  - Effort: M (new game loop + per-language word-pair curation × 5 languages)
  - Risk: medium — Hebrew/Japanese word-pair curation is non-trivial

## What other word-game sites do well

- **NYT Crossplay** (`nytimes.com/games/crossplay`): post-game AI analysis ("Cross Bot") + in-game chat = first NYT game where social layer is built-in. Smart matchmaking by skill. This is the new ceiling for premium word game UX.
- **Nanagrams** (`nanagrams.io`): zero friction (no account, no ads), voice-in-room, live opponent grids, 4.8/5 rating. English-only is its ceiling — LexiClash's 5-language support is a direct moat if we close the social layer gap.
- **Connections Racer** (`connectionracer.com`): proof that "same puzzle, race format" is enough of a hook to build a standalone product. The mechanic requires zero rule explanation — if you know Connections, you know this.
- **Wordrace** (`bloob.io`): letter-claiming on a shared grid creates competitive reads you don't get in turn-based games. Good reference for how shared-resource tension works in real-time word play.
