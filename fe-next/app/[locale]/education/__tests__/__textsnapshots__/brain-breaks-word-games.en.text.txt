It feels like a reward, not a filler.Questions teachers askHow do I give students a join code?+When you start a class game on LexiClash, a code appears on your screen. Say the code aloud, or display it on the board. Students type or scan it on their phones. They join in 10 seconds. No login, no email, no barriers.What if some students don't have a phone?+They can pair with a neighbor, or you can run a round as a team game (one phone per group). Or save single-device games for when all devices are present. Brain breaks are flexible—no rule says it must be whole-class.Do I need to teach them how to play?+No. Game rules are on-screen. For the first round, walk through one example: "Find any word on the grid. Tap it. It scores points." Done. Most students understand in 30 seconds.Can I use a brain break if I teach a language other than English?+LexiClash supports six languages: English, Hebrew, Spanish, Swedish, Japanese, and Russian. Set your class language in the dashboard, and games use words in that language. You can also upload custom word lists in any subject.How long should a brain break be?+1–5 minutes works. Shorter is often better. A 2-minute game is enough to reset focus and can fit between any two activities. Longer (3–5 min) is better if students are very wound up and need sustained outlet.Will this distract students from the lesson?+Only if you make it indefinite. Set a clear end time before you start: "We play for 3 minutes, then phones away." When students know the boundary, they re-engage more easily. Brain breaks reset focus; they don't derail it if you control the clock.Can I use the same game twice in one week?+Yes. Repetition is fine—students often like playing the same game again to beat their own score. You can also upload a custom word list once and reuse it all week.What games are best for brain breaks?+Word Hunt (silent letter search), Word Wheel (spinning word finder), and quick Multiplayer rounds (1–2 min) work best. Avoid long tournaments or complex modes that need explanation.
