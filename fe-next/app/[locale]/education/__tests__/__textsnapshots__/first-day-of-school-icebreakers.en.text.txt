The pressure to "introduce yourself" is gone—you just see who they are in real time. Some kids talk less in round one, more in round two. That's how it works.Can I use this if students already know each other?+Yes. It builds names into faces, not just names into a list. Plus, a fresh class mix (even in repeat years) creates enough distance that the low-pressure intro still lands.What grade levels does this work for?+Elementary through high school. Adjust the language: younger kids hunt simpler words; older kids hunt longer words or play with a theme filter. One game, many speeds.Can I play this asynchronously?+Yes. Give students a join code, a prompt (e.g., "Find words about summer"), and a deadline. They play on their own time. Less icebreaker energy, more asynchronous see-what-they-know.What if a student doesn't have a device?+Pair them with a neighbor, or they help that pair hunt. They're still known. Still part of the room.Can I use this for ESL or multilingual classes?+Absolutely. Younger learners hunt shorter words; advanced learners hunt by meaning. Post the prompt in English or the home language.How do I keep it moving?+Set a timer. Two minutes per round, not ten. Kids move fast once they get the shape of the game.
