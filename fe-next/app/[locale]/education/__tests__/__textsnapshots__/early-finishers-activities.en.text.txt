No "Is this right?" questions.Questions about early finishersWill they rush through their main work just to get to the game?+Some will, at first. Set clear rules: "You must finish the work properly before you play." Use a checklist so they know what "finished" means. After a week, it becomes normal. Most teachers say the game actually motivates slower workers because they see peers playing.Is it safe? Any chat or outside links?+No chat, no DMs, no external links. It's a word game only. Students see other players' names and scores on the leaderboard, but that's all. No personal information is shared.What about screen time?+Each round is 2–4 minutes. If a student finishes 10 minutes early, they play one round, not six. The game stops them when the timer ends. It's shorter than a recess video.Do I need to teach them how to play?+Two-minute demo with the whole class. Show them the grid, point to a word, let them find one. That's enough. They learn by playing. No tutorial needed.What if I have no devices in the room?+You need at least one device (a phone or tablet) for early finishers to share. Many classrooms have a class set of Chromebooks or a shared iPad. LexiClash works on all of them.Can I use the same code all term, or do I need a new one each week?+Same code all term. Students don't need to remember it—you write it on the board or a card once, and it stays there. They use it every time they finish early.Will I see what they played?+Yes. Your teacher dashboard shows scores, which words they found, and which they missed. Use that to pick vocabulary for next week's lesson.What if early finishers are just a distraction, not a learning gap?+Give them harder words or set a higher score goal. LexiClash adapts to different levels. Or rotate them: some play the word game, others read, others do maths.
